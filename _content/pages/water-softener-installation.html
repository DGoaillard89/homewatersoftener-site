If you live in London, your water's almost certainly hard. Most of it comes in at around 250–300+ mg/l as calcium carbonate, because it's spent time soaking through chalk and limestone before it gets to you. A whole-house water softener sorts that out where the mains comes in, so every tap gets softened water (apart from one drinking tap, which we leave hard on purpose). We fit them all over London and the Home Counties, from our base in Chiswick.

Why bother with a softener?

Hard water won't hurt you. It's just a pain to live with. The calcium and magnesium in it are what turn into limescale when water's heated or left to dry. Take them out and you'll notice it fast:

  • Less limescale. No more crusty taps, cloudy shower screens or bits floating in your tea.
  • Happier appliances. Scale builds up inside boilers, cylinders, kettles, washing machines and dishwashers. Soft water stops new scale forming.
  • Less cleaning. The descaler can go to the back of the cupboard.
  • Nicer-feeling water. Soap and shampoo lather properly, and plenty of people say their skin and hair feel softer. Plenty of families find soft water more comfortable at bath time. It's not a treatment for any skin condition, though, so if skin's a worry, have a chat with your GP or health visitor.

Most installs are done in a day. Typically:

  • A hard drinking tap. We fit a separate kitchen tap with unsoftened water for drinking and cooking.

Before we go, we'll set it up for your water, check for leaks and show you how to top up the salt and use the bypass.

Choosing the right system

We don't push one brand. The right softener depends on your house and how you use water. Here are the main choices:

  • Metered or timer. A metered softener regenerates based on how much water you've actually used, so it suits busy, changeable households. A timer unit regenerates on a fixed schedule. Simpler, but it can waste a bit of salt and water.
  • Non-electric or electric. Non-electric units are usually compact, run on block salt and don't need a socket. Electric ones usually take tablet salt and have a control panel. Our guide to block salt vs tablets vs granular goes into the pros and cons.
  • Size. We size it to how many people live there, how many bathrooms you've got and how hard your water is. A typical three-bed terrace needs less than a big family house with three showers going at 7.30am.

What about drinking water?

Softening swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium, roughly 46 mg/l of sodium for every 100 mg/l of hardness taken out. With London water, that can get close to the UK legal limit of 200 mg/l. It's also not recommended for baby formula. So we fit an unsoftened drinking tap with every softener. If you'd like filtered, low-sodium water instead, reverse osmosis with its own kitchen tap is another option. See our drinking water options and can you drink softened water?

Frequently asked questions

How long does a water softener install take?

Most are done in a day. We'll confirm at the survey.

Where will the softener go?

Usually under the kitchen sink, or in a utility room, garage or cupboard near the mains stopcock. It needs to be on the incoming mains, near a drain.

Do I need a plug socket?

Not always. Non-electric block-salt softeners don't need power at all. Electric tablet-salt units need a socket nearby.

Can I still drink the tap water?

Yes, from the drinking tap. We fit a separate unsoftened tap in the kitchen with every softener, so you've got hard water for drinking, cooking and baby formula. That follows the Drinking Water Inspectorate's advice to keep an unsoftened kitchen tap.

What happens at the free survey?

We test your water, find where the mains comes in, look at where the softener could go and check the drain and power options. We'll also ask how many people live there and how many bathrooms you have, so the unit's the right size. Then you get a fixed quote in writing.

Does my water supplier need to know about the install?

Yes. Salt-based softeners are on the list in regulation 5 of the Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999, so the water supplier has to be told before the work starts. They then have ten working days to respond. We'll go through it with you when we quote.
